# External credential profile: vc-di-ecdsa-rdfc-2019@1

**Status: working draft, pre-1.0.** This document defines one additional credential representation beside the native record and the VSC SEAL: a passport credential another party issued in the W3C Verifiable Credentials Data Model 2.0, secured with a Data Integrity proof under the `ecdsa-rdfc-2019` cryptosuite over a P-256 Multikey, and committed on chain by its exact bytes. It defines what a verifier checks, how each finding is reported, and what is deliberately outside the profile. It changes nothing about the native record, the native claim, the generic anchor or the VSC profile; [`exchange.md`](exchange.md) registers the representation and [`verification.md`](verification.md) fixes the report every finding lands in.

## 1. Scope

The profile is `vc-di-ecdsa-rdfc-2019@1`, manifest `packages/dpp-profiles/manifests/exchange/vc-di-ecdsa-rdfc-2019@1.json`, representation identifier `vc-di-ecdsa-rdfc-2019@1`, media type `application/vc+ld+json`. It is a verification and intake profile: this repository issues nothing under it, and a credential it accepts remains that issuer's statement, retained as evidence beside the passport. Verification is implemented by `@bsv/vsc/exchange`, a separate entry point from the SEAL verifier, so a foreign credential is never read under the SEAL profile's context allowlist or custody rules and the main entry's verifier is unchanged.

The mandatory support is: VC Data Model 2.0; an issuer that is a `did:web` identifier; exactly one credential subject with an explicit URI identifier; a proof of type `DataIntegrityProof`, cryptosuite `ecdsa-rdfc-2019`, purpose `assertionMethod`, whose verification method is a `Multikey` with a P-256 public key (`publicKeyMultibase` beginning `zDn`) controlled by the issuer and listed under `assertionMethod` in the issuer's document. Other curves, other cryptosuites, other proof types, proof sets that mix suites, multiple subjects, issuer objects and other DID methods are reported as `unsupported`. Unsupported is a distinct answer from failed: a P-384 proof the maintained suite could verify is still not a verified credential under this profile.

## 2. Exact bytes, and what a proof does not commit to

The verifier takes the credential as the exact UTF-8 bytes it was issued as. Parsing is strict: duplicate keys, malformed UTF-8, nesting beyond the profile depth and input above 256 KiB are refused before any interpretation, and the parsed object is never re-serialised as if it were the original. The SHA-256 of those bytes is the profile's exact-byte digest, and it is what the generic anchor of [`rules.md`](rules.md) §5 commits to when the credential is anchored: `representation` `vc-di-ecdsa-rdfc-2019@1`, `mediaType` `application/vc+ld+json`, `attestationId` the credential's `id`, `issuer` its issuer, `subject` its one `credentialSubject.id`, `attestationType` the credential's first type after `VerifiableCredential`.

An `ecdsa-rdfc-2019` proof is computed over the RDF canonicalisation of the credential, not over its bytes. The same credential re-serialised with different whitespace or key order still verifies, and has a different exact-byte digest. The two facts are reported independently and neither implies the other: a verified proof does not make a re-serialised copy the anchored representation, and a matching digest does not make a credential valid. A transformed document or a newly issued projection has its own identity, its own proof if any, and its own anchor; it inherits neither from the original.

## 3. Contexts and schema

A credential's `@context` must be an array of strings equal, exactly and in order, to one of the context sets the operator's policy pins. The default sets are the VC 2.0 context alone, the VC 2.0 context with the Data Integrity v2 context, and either followed by the owned payload context `urn:bsv:dpp:external-passport:context:1`. Every pinned context resolves to recorded bytes: the W3C contexts from the maintained context packages, with the VC 2.0 bytes carried beside the package and held equal by a test, and the owned context from the package's own artefact. Nothing is fetched from a URL a credential names. An inline context object is unsupported, because it could redefine a signed term; a context set the policy has not pinned is unsupported; and a proof over unpinned contexts is not evaluated. Canonicalisation runs in safe mode, so a term no pinned context defines fails the proof rather than vanishing.

The schema check holds the credential to the owned structural schema `urn:bsv:dpp:external-passport:schema:1` by default, or to the schema the operator's policy names for a payload profile. It is a shape check and nothing else: it says the credential has one identified subject, a DID issuer, a validity start and a proof, and it says nothing about the meaning or applicability of what the subject carries. Industry validation of an external credential's payload is a separate selection under the industry profile, and an unknown payload schema may permit proof inspection without passing schema or industry validation.

## 4. Issuer binding, proof, time, status and authority

The verifier resolves the issuer's DID document through the pinned document loader or, where the operator configured one, through a bounded `did:web` resolver limited to permitted origins with no redirects and a byte cap. The document must identify the issuer. Each proof's verification method must resolve to a `Multikey` controlled by the issuer and listed under the document's `assertionMethod`, by identifier or embedded; a key the issuer holds but never authorised for assertion fails the binding, and the suite refuses the proof for its purpose. The proof is then verified by the maintained cryptosuite with the resolved document as controller. Resolving a document establishes a key relationship and nothing more: it does not prove the issuer is entitled to a GS1 identifier, accredited for a claim, or the party the subject names.

The validity interval is evaluated at a stated evaluation time under the same rules as the VSC profile: not yet valid and expired are distinct failures, and a credential without `validFrom` is a failure. Status uses the W3C Bitstring Status List through the same bounded mechanism as the VSC profile, with the list's proof verified under this profile's suite: the list must carry the identifier the entry names, be issued by the credential issuer or by a list issuer the verifier's policy has explicitly delegated, verify, be within its own validity, be no older than the policy's signed-freshness bound (at most seven days), carry a canonical encoding decompressed within a bound, and hold the index. A set bit is `revoked` or `suspended` by purpose. A credential without a status entry fails when the policy requires status, is indeterminate when no status policy is configured, and is accepted with a recorded limitation only when the operator selected the profile option that permits it. A list that cannot be retrieved leaves status and availability indeterminate; a freshly fetched old list stays old.

Issuer authority is a named policy the operator configures independently of any credential: either a statement that no external authority is required, reported as `not-required` with its reason, or a list of trusted issuers for the subject and claim, reported as verified or as `issuer_unauthorised`. A credential cannot introduce its own trusted issuer, trust anchor or registry; anything of the kind it carries is not read.

## 5. Subject binding

The credential names one subject. When the verifier is given an expected passport identifier, the binding is verified only if the signed subject equals it, or if a named subject-binding policy carries an established binding from that subject to that passport with an evidence reference, which the finding then cites. A different identifier is `subject_mismatch`; nothing is inferred from matching text, a shared serial, an account association or an unsigned request parameter. Without an expectation the finding is `not-required` and names the subject, so the report's own subject-binding check can compare it. A serial under a different GTIN is not the same subject.

## 6. The report

The verifier's findings map onto the sixteen-check report of [`verification.md`](verification.md) through the representation's credential verifier: the proof and issuer-binding findings answer `externalCredentialProof` (a proof whose key its issuer never authorised does not verify under its suite's controller rule, so the two answer together, worst first); the schema finding answers `schema`; the validity interval answers `credentialTime`; the status finding answers `credentialStatus` with the purpose and value in its detail; the authority finding answers `issuerAuthority`; and unresolved documents answer `evidenceAvailability`. The subject binding is answered by the report itself from the subjects the verifier returns and the expectation the caller supplied. Reason codes are the report's shared codes: `suite-unsupported` for an unsupported context set, curve, suite or structure; `signature-invalid` for a failed proof or an unauthorised key; `proof-absent`; `schema-invalid`; `not-yet-valid` and `expired`; `status-revoked`, `status-suspended`, `status-stale`, `status-unknown`, `status-unauthenticated`, `status-unset`; `policy-missing`; `authority-unconfirmed` and `authority-not-required`; `subject-mismatch`; `referenced-artefact-unavailable`; `representation-unsupported`. No aggregate verdict is derived from them.

## 7. Intake and anchoring

A registry that accepts this representation receives the exact issued bytes inside its explicit representation envelope, verifies them under this profile with its configured policy, and retains the bytes unchanged beside their metadata and intake verification. It anchors accepted credentials through the existing generic anchor on the existing attestation topic; no new topic or wire format is introduced, and the anchor's issuer, subject, credential identifier, representation and media type must match the secured document. A credential whose identifier is already secured under different bytes is refused, never replaced. A credential that fails a required check stays outside accepted and anchored intake; its inspection result may be retained separately and labelled as such. Anchoring a credential commits the anchoring service to its bytes and says nothing about the truth of its claims, the issuer's authority, or any token operation: an external passport credential is retained evidence and drives no Issue, Update, Transfer or Retire.

## 8. Fixtures

`fixtures/vectors/dpp/interoperability/external-credential/v1.json` carries independently constructed vectors: a positive credential with its issuer document, keys and status list; the same credential re-serialised, whose proof holds and whose digest differs; a tampered value; an inline context substitution; an unauthorised key; a substituted issuer; expired and not-yet-valid intervals; a revoked bit; a list from another issuer; an unavailable list; a P-384 key; a `ecdsa-jcs-2019` proof; two subjects; a subject bound only by policy; a subject mismatch; the two authority policies; the optional-status limitation; and a credential identifier collision pair for intake. The key scalars are published in the positive vector so a reader in any language rebuilds the same keys; nothing derived from them will ever hold value.

## 9. Normative and implementation

What a conforming party reproduces: the exact-byte commitment and its independence from proof validity (§2), the pinned context sets and safe canonicalisation (§3), the issuer binding, suite restriction, temporal, status and named-authority rules (§4), the subject-binding rule (§5), the separate reporting of every finding under the shared reason codes (§6), and the intake and anchoring rules (§7). What is a build's own: which context sets and payload schemas its policy pins, which issuers it trusts, whether it resolves `did:web` live or from pinned documents, and where it stores the four artefacts of [`exchange.md`](exchange.md) §3.
