# Portable evidence: complete pages and packages that survive their operator

**Status: working draft, pre-1.0.** This document defines how a reader retrieves a complete history from a service in pages that do not lie about completeness, and how the evidence for a passport travels as a signed package that another operator, or a reader with no operator at all, can verify. The property it serves is [`services.md`](services.md) §1: no service is load-bearing, so what a service holds must be exportable, complete relative to a stated scope, and verifiable from bytes and headers once it has left.

## 1. Pages of a snapshot

A bounded query that returns the newest five hundred records and stops is a lookup, not an export. An export answers in pages over a stable snapshot, and each page says what it is.

| Property | Requirement |
|---|---|
| `items` | The page's records, in the snapshot's order. |
| `nextCursor` | An opaque cursor for the next page, or `null` when the snapshot is exhausted. |
| `snapshotId` | The identifier of the snapshot every page of this export is read from. |
| `scope` | What was queried, in words and identifiers: the topics or services, the passport or issuer selectors, the source's own sequence range. |
| `truncated` | `true` when the page was cut short by a limit, `false` otherwise. |
| `completeForSnapshot` | `true` only on the last page and only when every record in the snapshot's scope has been returned; never on an earlier page, and never a statement about records outside the snapshot. |

Ordering is by the source's own sequence, then by transaction and output identity, so two readers of one snapshot receive the same pages. Cursors are opaque, bound to the query and the snapshot, and refused with a named error when tampered with or expired; an expired snapshot answers a named restart, never a silent continuation from the middle. The default page holds 100 items and the maximum 500, and there is no hidden cap on the total. Completeness is relative to the snapshot's scope: an export can be complete for what one source held and still miss what another source holds, and a reader checks token predecessor continuity and reports missing evidence independently of page completion. The existing bounded lookups remain readable for what they are, documented as bounded, and are not used as exports.

## 2. The evidence package

`dpp-evidence-package@1` is a directory or archive of content-addressed files under a signed manifest conforming to [`../contracts/evidence-package.schema.json`](../contracts/evidence-package.schema.json).

The manifest names the format and version, the exact passport identifier, genesis outpoint and selected tip, the export time, the profile and policy references under which the package was assembled, the source observations it rests on (which services were asked, when, and what they said about the latest state), the disclosure scope (public only, or which restricted tiers are included and for whom), the completeness declarations (complete for which source snapshots, and what is known to be absent or withheld), and an inventory of every file: its path, media type, byte length and SHA-256, in one of the categories below.

| Category | What it holds |
|---|---|
| `transactions` | Raw transactions of the token history, and of anchors. |
| `proofs` | BEEF and BUMP material: merkle paths and the ancestors a verifier needs. |
| `native-claims` | Signed native lifecycle claims, as posted. |
| `external-credentials` | Original secured credentials in their exact bytes, with their representation and media type. |
| `evidence` | Evidence files the claims reference, encrypted where restricted; and the retained originals of an EPCIS exchange, in their exact bytes (below). |
| `schemas` | The schemas and contexts the credentials were validated against, pinned; and the files of a profile bundle, which carry the definitions of the profiles the records declare (below). |
| `authority` | Historical publisher and authority records: which keys were authorised when, under which policy; and the acceptance records the lineage's `TRANSFER`s commit to under [`managed-custody.md`](managed-custody.md) §3, one file per record with `representation` the record's own `acceptanceFormat`, `dpp-managed-acceptance@2` or the superseded `dpp-managed-acceptance@1`, and `represents` the commitment as `sha256:<hex>`. |
| `status` | The status observations made at export, with their retrieval times and the documents read. |
| `reports` | Verification reports produced at export, in the shape [`verification.md`](verification.md) defines; and the durable records of an EPCIS exchange with the descriptor of its snapshot (below). |

Paths are relative, contain no traversal and no duplicates, and decompression of any archive form is bounded. The manifest signature is ECDSA over the SHA-256 of the manifest's canonical JSON ([`rules.md`](rules.md) §4) without the signature value. Every exporter, an index or a registry, signs under one model: `exporter.id` is the exporter's identity key, which its capability document names, and `signature.signer` is that key's BRC-42 child for protocol `[1, 'dpp evidence package v1']`, key identifier the passport identifier and counterparty `anyone`, so a reader derives the signer from the identity key alone, and a signature made for one passport does not verify under the key derived for another. A package whose `signature.signer` equals its `exporter.id` was signed directly with that raw key, as an index on an earlier release signs; it keeps verifying under that key, which a reader obtains from the operator and never from the package, and passes as the expected signer. The signature authenticates the exporter and the inventory: that this exporter assembled these bytes. It does not prove that the inventory is exhaustive or that any included claim is true; a reader verifies every digest, every transaction, every proof and every claim independently of who signed the package. Absent or restricted material is declared, never implied present; a `TRANSFER` whose acceptance record the exporter does not hold is declared absent by its commitment. A package assembled under a public disclosure scope is not a recovery backup and says so. Credentials and secrets that authorise spending are never part of an evidence package; custody recovery has its own governed process.

A package MAY carry the definitions of the profiles its records declare, as a profile bundle ([`profiles.md`](profiles.md) §7): each binding in its exact bytes, every artefact the binding lists, the notices that travel with them and a descriptor. A bundle uses the inventory as it is and adds no member to the manifest. Every file of a bundle is one entry in the `schemas` category. Its `representation` is `dpp-profile-bundle@1` for the descriptor, `dpp-profile-binding@1` for a binding file, `dpp-profile-artefact@1` for an artefact, `dpp-profile-notice@1` for a notice or `dpp-profile-licence-text@1` for a licence text. Its `represents` is the profile as `id@version` for a binding file, the profile and the file's path in its binding as `id@version#path` for an artefact or a notice, and the licence's name for a licence text. The manifest's `profiles` names the profiles carried. A file that is named and not carried, because its publisher's terms do not let others pass it on, is one line of `completeness.withheld`, written `provisioned-by-destination <address> sha256:<hex> bytes:<length>`, and its bytes are in no file of the package. The exporter's signature covers these entries as it covers every other and says the same of them: that this exporter assembled these bytes. It gives no binding any authority. A reader restores a profile from a package only through the registration rules of [`profiles.md`](profiles.md) §7, against the binding digests its own operator trusts, so a package that is signed correctly and carries a binding the reader does not trust is refused for that binding.

A package MAY carry a snapshot of an EPCIS exchange ([`epcis-interoperability.md`](epcis-interoperability.md) section 8). It also uses the inventory as it is and adds no member to the manifest. Every retained original is one entry in the `evidence` category, with `representation` `dpp-epcis-original@1`, the media type it was received under and `represents` its content address as `sha256:<hex>`. Every durable record is one entry in the `reports` category: `representation` is `dpp-epcis-import@2` for an import record, `dpp-epcis-observation@1`, `dpp-epcis-conflict-resolution@1`, `dpp-epcis-pull@1` or `dpp-epcis-pull-page@1` for the other records, and `dpp-epcis-public-import@1` for an import record as a public package carries it, and `represents` is the record's own identity. The descriptor, `epcis-recovery.json`, is one more `reports` entry with `representation` `dpp-epcis-recovery@1`, conforming to [`../contracts/epcis-recovery.schema.json`](../contracts/epcis-recovery.schema.json); it names every file the snapshot needs, so a snapshot missing one of them is refused. A snapshot covers the source scopes its descriptor names, whatever passport the carrying package is about, and each scope is one entry of `completeness.snapshots`. Everything a snapshot leaves out is one line of `completeness.withheld`, written `epcis-exchange <what> <reason>`. The two access paths stay apart here as everywhere: an authorised recovery snapshot, which carries the operational records, travels only in a package whose disclosure is `all-tiers` and that declares itself a recovery backup, and a package under a public disclosure carries only the public form, which holds no operational record, no provenance and no cursor and cannot be restored. Neither holds a credential, a key or an address that carries a secret. The exporter's signature says of these entries what it says of every other: that this exporter assembled these bytes. A reader restores a snapshot only after the checks of [`epcis-interoperability.md`](epcis-interoperability.md) section 8.

A package MAY carry the evidence of more than one passport, as a complete export of an application does. It is still one package under one manifest and adds no member to it. Its `passportId` then names the export and not a passport: `urn:bsv:dpp:evidence-export:sha256:<hex>`, where the digest is the SHA-256 of the canonical JSON ([`rules.md`](rules.md) §4) of the list of the passport identifiers the package carries, each once, in ascending order of UTF-16 code units. The same selection has the same name, and because the signer is derived from that name, a signature made for one selection verifies for no other. `genesis` and `selectedTip` are absent from such a manifest. Each passport's own evidence sits in a folder of its own, as the package its exporter signed: that package's manifest is one `reports` entry with `representation` `dpp-evidence-package@1` and `represents` the passport identifier, and each of its files is one entry in the category its own manifest gives it, at the same path under the folder. A reader that wants one passport inspects that folder as the package it is, under its own exporter's key and that passport's identifier. The outer signature says of these entries what it says of every other, that the outer exporter assembled these bytes, and it does not stand in for the inner one. A reader that expects a package about one passport refuses an export by its `passportId`, and a reader of exports refuses a package whose name is not the one its carried passports give. The two access paths stay apart: an export that carries restricted records declares `all-tiers` and that it is a recovery backup, and an export under the public disclosure carries none of them.

A single package MAY be bounded, and a bounded package declares it: the states it does not carry are absent by outpoint and its snapshot declaration is not complete. A reader that needs a lineage longer than an exporter's bound asks that exporter for more, by whatever further route it documents; the multi-part export of the reference index package is such a route, a feature of that package and not part of this standard.

## 3. Replicas, recovery and restricted evidence

An operator that claims durable publication keeps an independently administered replica of the required evidence. The reference deployment proposes a recovery point objective of zero for evidence it has acknowledged as immutable and a recovery time objective of twenty-four hours, to be demonstrated with the chosen storage systems and stated as service objectives, not as regulatory periods; retention duration comes from the applicable industry or legal profile. While replication of a record is pending, the operator distinguishes blockchain acceptance from completed durable publication rather than claiming both.

Restricted evidence is encrypted at rest and in every package that carries it, and authorised access is tested after an operator migration, including key rotation, recovery, the withdrawal of a former recipient's access and the loss of the original account provider. Public digest checks continue when restricted content is unavailable: a digest cannot restore missing bytes, and deleting a stored row cannot erase metadata already published on chain.

## 4. Normative and implementation

What a conforming party reproduces: the page properties and their meanings, the snapshot ordering and the cursor and expiry rules (§1); the package manifest, its inventory categories, the entries a profile bundle and an EPCIS exchange snapshot are listed under, how a package that carries more than one passport is named and laid out, its signing model, the digest-first import and the declarations of absence (§2); and the separation of blockchain acceptance from durable publication (§3). What is a build's own: the storage behind a snapshot, the archive form, the bound of a package and any further route past it, where a replica runs, and how a deployment meets the objectives it declares.
