# Changelog

**Pre-1.0.** Everything below is the working draft assembling itself. Breaking changes are expected and are recorded here without apology until version 1.0 is declared, as `GOVERNANCE.md` states. Entries name the pull request that landed them; a change to a wire format names the fixture that moved with it, because the two are never allowed to drift apart.

## Unreleased

### 2026-08-30

- `spec/writing.md`, the seventh normative document (#33): what a writer owes the record, as outcomes. The verifier's own check before anything is sent; announce before sending; one writer per passport and no state reported as written before the network answers; broadcast through the wallet and through more than one door; a failed announcement never fails a record and is retried rather than rebuilt; the merkle path obtained, kept and offered to the index; the bytes retained for the passport's life. A personal wallet discharges the broadcast, proof and retention duties itself. `services.md` gains the index's duty to serve mined states with their proofs and the ways it can learn of mining (re-announcing is not one, because admission of a held output is a no-op); `record-model.md` §8 makes the header source a configuration and says the proof is obtained once and travels with the record; the rationale and governance (writer conformance is self-reported, one sentence per rule) cross-reference the new document.
- `contracts/overlay.yaml` 0.3.0-draft: `POST /arc-ingest` moves from may to should, with the proof checked to contain the transaction and validated against the header source before it is stored, and is documented as a path with its answers (#33).
- `@bsv/dpp-overlay-topics` serves `POST /arc-ingest` in the shape an ARC-compatible callback carries; the lookup then answers the state with its merkle path. `ARC_CALLBACK_TOKEN` guards it as a bearer or `X-Callback-Token` (#33).
- `examples/write-passport.mjs`: the writer's lifecycle from one BRC-100 wallet under possession, with a dry run CI runs that rebuilds the fixture's first state byte for byte and shows the writer's own check refusing a forbidden state before it could be sent (#33).
- `docs/stack.md` gains the writing section and the proof-ingestion follow-ups; `docs/deployment.md` names the deployment that follows the standard's defaults, the one place the ecosystem's endpoints appear (#33).
- `spec/custody.md`, the sixth normative document (#32): the four keys a state involves and their roles, the constraint that every role is fillable by a BRC-100 wallet with one derivation and no key export, the pinned convention that a lock held by the owner is the field-6 key, the optional owner-signed transfer a profile may select (equality or `owner_linkage`, with transfer authorities for recovery), and the three custody arrangements with their costs, stated as peers. `record-model.md` gains the field-6 derivation as a SHOULD, reserves `owner_linkage` on `TRANSFER` and names the optional eighth invariant; the rationale records the single-key lock as a decision; identity, services and the overlay profile cross-reference the new document.
- `@bsv/dpp-core` gains `owner.ts` (the owner key, the linkage scalar, its BRC-100 revelation and decryption, the pure linkage check and the consent predicate), `verifyChain`'s `ownerConsent` option and `StateCheck.ownerConsentValid`, a required property of every result and so a pre-1.0 type change (#32).
- `fixtures/chain-v1.json` grows two states, the first locked to the owner's per-passport key and the second spent from the owner's wallet with `owner_linkage` in `event_data`, and seven consent refusals that are valid chains with the option off; `fixtures/vectors/dpp/chain/v1.json` is the chain's vector form, the first generated by a test and held identical (#32).
- `@bsv/dpp-overlay-topics`: `tm_dpp` takes the owner-signed transfer as an admission option, configured by `OWNER_CONSENT` and `TRANSFER_AUTHORITIES`, and documents per instance whether it enforces it; `examples/verify-passport.mjs` takes `--owner-consent` and CI runs that form (#32).
- The standard is aligned with the BSV TypeScript stack, one commit per component (#31). `spec/record-model.md` names the output a BRC-48 PushDrop and says why a generic decoder is not a conforming reader, spells out the BRC-42/43 invoice numbers, bounds `passport_id` at 512 bytes and `actor_keyID` at 256 because both are BRC-42 key identifiers, and recommends UHRP hosting and AES-256-GCM under a wallet-derived key for the owner tier. `@bsv/dpp-core` enforces the bounds and `fixtures/record-v1.json` gains the `overlongPassportId` refusal vector.
- `spec/rules.md` names the anchor a BRC-48 PushDrop, spells out both invoice numbers, bounds the claim's `passportId` at 512 and defines the preimage's VarInt; `spec/identity.md` cites `@bsv/did` and the canonical-key check it lacks, and names `did:bsv` and `did:web` (#31).
- `contracts/overlay.yaml` becomes a profile of ts-stack's overlay HTTP contract with an `x-dpp-profile` block of must, should and may; `spec/services.md` adds SHIP/SLAP discoverability, overlay-first announcement and idempotent admission (#31).
- `fixtures/vectors/`: the same bytes in the stack's cross-language conformance vector format, 25 vectors validated by the stack's runner, with the test private keys published and four anchor refusals the bespoke file lacked (#31).
- `docs/stack.md`: the map from every rule to the stack component behind it, and the follow-ups that belong in other repositories; `GOVERNANCE.md` rule 3 counts both fixture forms (#31).
