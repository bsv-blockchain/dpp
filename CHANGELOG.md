# Changelog

This file records what a consumer of the `@bsv/dpp-*` packages, the contracts and the fixtures needs: the changes in the unpublished candidate and the releases already published. Every package is an experimental beta prerelease whose API may change significantly. The standard is pre-1.0, so breaking changes are expected and recorded here without apology until version 1.0 is declared, as `GOVERNANCE.md` states. [Migration](docs/migration.md) gives the steps for each change that needs one.

## Unpublished: dpp-release-2026-11

`dpp-release-2026-11` is the current candidate: `@bsv/dpp-protocol` and `@bsv/dpp-profiles` `0.3.0-beta.10`, `@bsv/dpp-overlay` `0.4.0-beta.13`, `@bsv/dpp-credentials` `0.2.0-beta.6` and `@bsv/create-dpp-app` `0.1.0-beta.2`. Each line below is a change since the last publication, `dpp-release-2026-10-10` on 16 October 2026. A step number is a step of [test the November source candidate](docs/migration.md#test-the-november-source-candidate).

- The W3C credentials context in `@bsv/dpp-credentials` now ships with the full W3C Software and Document License text and its copyright notice in `artifacts/w3c/NOTICE.md`, and the index image carries `NOTICE` and `PATENTS` beside `LICENSE`. No API changes.
- `managed-custody@2` is the one custody profile: readers read `dpp-managed-acceptance@1` under its direct-signature rule and `@2` under the derived key, and nothing writes `@1` (step 1).
- `signManagedAcceptance` takes the state-signing `DataSigner` in place of the removed `AcceptanceSigner` interface and writes `dpp-managed-acceptance@2` only, signed by the custodian's BRC-42 child for `[1, 'dpp managed record v1']`; `MANAGED_CUSTODY_PROFILE` is `managed-custody@2`, which the index declares, and `MANAGED_RECORD_PROTOCOL_ID`, `managedRecordVerificationKey`, `MANAGED_ACCEPTANCE_FORMAT_V1` and `MANAGED_CUSTODY_PROFILE_V1` are new (step 2).
- Under `managed-custody@2` each state locks to its own field-6 controller key (`spec/managed-custody.md` §2).
- The owner-signed transfer, `owner-consent@1`, is removed: `checkOwnerConsent`, `OWNER_CONSENT_REFUSALS`, `OWNER_LINKAGE_KEY`, the `ownerConsent` option and `ownerConsentValid` are gone, `ownerConsentPolicy` leaves `@bsv/dpp-overlay/server`, the index drops the `consent-not-proven` refusal and the capability document's `ownerConsent` and `transferAuthorities`, and control authorities default to none (step 7).
- `normaliseTransferAuthorities` is `normaliseControlAuthorities`, the `ChainFailureKind` `consent` is `commitment` and `StateInspection.consentError` is `commitmentError` (step 7).
- The holder instruction record is parked outside the standard: a custodian's `UPDATE` or `RETIRE` for a managed holder after a committed `TRANSFER` depends on custody, and the report's limits say so (step 3).
- The index refuses to boot with `OWNER_CONSENT`, `TRANSFER_AUTHORITIES`, `INSTRUCTION_COMMITMENT` or `EXPORT_SIGNING_KEY` set: recovery keys go in `CONTROL_AUTHORITIES` and the export key in `EXPORTER_PRIVATE_KEY` (steps 3 and 9).
- Record version 3, the token carrier, is parked: the packages read and write versions 1 and 2 only, every version 3 export is gone, and the index drops the `carrier-prefix-invalid` and `token-id-mismatch` refusals and the `tokenId` lookup and declares no `dpp-record` version `3` (step 8).
- The verification report is version 2, without the reason codes `carrier-prefix-invalid`, `token-id-mismatch`, `consent-not-proven` and `resource-limit`; a version 1 report validates under `verification-report-v1.schema.json` (step 11).
- `verifyPassportEvidence` checks each token state's `payload_public` against its declared profile through the policy's `payloadValidator`, which `profilePayloadValidator(ajv)` from `@bsv/dpp-profiles` supplies; the new reasons are `profile-undeclared` and `schema-unavailable` (step 4).
- The `payloadValidator` is told which passport a payload sits in: `DeclaredPayload` gains `passportId`, read from the decoded state and never from the payload, the expected subject or the outpoint, and `PayloadToValidate` in `@bsv/dpp-profiles` gains an optional `passportId` that `profilePayloadValidator` ignores. A validator that ignores it works as before, and no transaction field, record format or signature preimage changes.
- `@bsv/dpp-profiles` recognises a profile defined outside the package and held by an immutable binding, beside the manifest format (`spec/profiles.md` §7, `schemas/profile-binding-v1.schema.json`). `createProfileRegistry({ trustedBindingDigests })` registers a binding only when its digest is trusted, no other binding answers for its `id` and `version`, and every artefact it names matches its recorded byte length and SHA-256; it refuses with a typed code and resolves the supported manifest profiles through the same `resolve(id, version)`. New beside it: `readProfileBinding`, `profileBindingProblems`, `profileBindingInconsistencies`, `profileBindingDigest`, `describeArtefact` and `parseStrictJson`.
- `bindingPayloadValidator({ registry, engines })` in `@bsv/dpp-profiles` has the shape of `profilePayloadValidator` and of the report's `payloadValidator`. For a profile held by a binding it checks the payload envelope (`profile`, `profile_version`, `profile_binding_sha256`, `product`, and the optional `notice`), the binding digest, then the binding's `json-schema`, `json-ld-expand`, `shacl` and `passport-identity` steps. A step that fails is `fail`; a step that could not run, for want of an engine, a capability, time, a registered profile or a passport identifier, is `unknown` and never `pass`. The answer keeps the reason codes `schema-invalid` and `schema-unavailable`, and `PayloadValidation.detail` may now be a `BindingValidationDetail` that names the precise cause and lists every step and SHACL finding.
- `checkProductIdentity` in `@bsv/dpp-profiles` decides whether the product document in a payload is the item its passport is about: the expected identity is the supplied `passportId` alone, the host is not compared, GTIN widths are equivalent, a serial is compared exactly, and a model, a batch, a contradiction or a wrong check digit fails.
- `@bsv/dpp-profiles/validation-engine` is a new entry point: `createValidationEngine` builds the JSON-LD and SHACL engines, with SPARQL constraints, over `jsonld@9.0.0`, `n3@2.13.12` and `shacl-engine@1.1.3`. The caller installs those libraries; the package does not depend on them, its main entry point never loads them and the index image does not install them. The adapter refuses any context it was not handed, fetches nothing and discards a result that arrives after its time limit.
- `examples/custom-profile/` holds two example profiles written for this repository, `example-bottle@1` and `example-notebook@1`, with their vectors, and `node examples/custom-profile.mjs` registers them from their folders and checks every vector.
- `@bsv/dpp-profiles` ships two bindings under `bindings/`, `eu-battery@1` and `eu-textile@1`, for battery and textile passport data of one serialised item. Each runs JSON-LD expansion, SHACL with SPARQL constraints and the passport identity check, and only violations block. `BUNDLED_BINDINGS` and `BUNDLED_BINDING_DIGESTS` are the trusted digest list for them, and `registerBundledBindings(registry)` reads them from the package folder, holds each binding file to its pinned digest and registers it; importing the package reads none of the files. The contexts and shapes are definitions published by a third party under Apache-2.0, see the notice beside each binding. Each name states the data model a product document uses and claims no compliance with any law, and `battery@2` and `textile@2` are unchanged ([EU battery and textile bindings](docs/profiles/eu-sector-bindings.md)).
- A binding may state an `access` member: which top-level properties of the product document are public. Every other property is restricted, one the binding does not name included, and nothing is split inside a nested object or a list (`spec/profiles.md` §7). The list is in the binding, so the digest a payload declares commits to it, and a binding that restricts a member the identity rules read is refused as `binding-inconsistent`. `eu-battery@1` lists 46 public properties and `eu-textile@1` 54, so a `payload_public` under either carries the public properties only, and one that carries any other property fails as `restricted-property-disclosed` ([which properties are public](docs/profiles/eu-sector-bindings.md#which-properties-are-public)). A binding without `access` is unchanged.
- `prepareBoundPayload` in `@bsv/dpp-profiles` is the writer's call under a binding: it validates the whole product document, splits it, returns the public payload as the exact text to sign with the restricted part beside it, and holds that text to 65,535 bytes of UTF-8. It refuses with `validation-failed`, `validation-unavailable`, `public-payload-too-large`, `public-view-failed` or `profile-not-bound`, and issues, signs, encrypts and stores nothing. `splitProductByAccess` and `assembleProduct` cut and rejoin the two parts on their own, and a rejoin refuses with `part-not-an-object`, `join-key-missing`, `restricted-part-names-another-product`, `property-in-both-parts`, `restricted-property-in-public-part` or `public-property-in-restricted-part`.
- `bindingPayloadValidator` reads a public payload under a binding with `access` as a projection of the whole document. A rule that misses only a restricted property is withheld, not failed, and any withheld rule makes the answer `unknown` with the cause `restricted-tier-withheld`, never `pass`. `authorisedPayloadValidator` is new: it takes the public payload and the restricted part, rejoins them and runs every step on the whole, and parts that do not rejoin fail as `product-assembly-refused`. `BindingValidationDetail` gains `access`, with the view checked and the withheld rules, and the steps `access` and `assembly`. Neither view compares a restricted part with a record's `payload_owner_hash`: that stays the protocol's check, and a pass or a rejoin is no evidence of it.

- Evidence packages are signed by the BRC-42 child of the exporter's identity key for `[1, 'dpp evidence package v1']` and the passport identifier: `EVIDENCE_PACKAGE_PROTOCOL`, `evidencePackageSigner` and `evidencePackageSigningKey` are new, the capability document names `exporterIdentityKey`, and `startOverlayService` takes `exportIdentityKey` (step 9).

- `@bsv/dpp-credentials/epcis-exchange` is a new entry point: durable EPCIS source exchange over storage the caller supplies. `importEpcisSource` retains a file's exact bytes and an import record before it acknowledges, refuses an unsafe transport or a missing GS1 schema with no side effect, and retains a document that fails validation with its findings as `invalid`. The same `requestId` with the same bytes answers the same import and writes no second record; with other bytes it answers `request-conflict`. Each event is recorded as `new`, `duplicate`, `new-observation` or `conflict` within its `sourceId`, and nothing overwrites a source. New beside it: `exportEpcisSource`, which returns the retained original byte for byte, `deriveEpcisEventProjection`, whose result is labelled `derived` and carries its own digest, `getEpcisConflict` and `resolveEpcisConflict`, `findEpcisAssociations` and `rebuildEpcisAssociations`, `recoverEpcisExchange`, `checkEpcisExchangeInvariants`, `getEpcisImport`, `findEpcisImportByRequest` and `listEpcisImports`. Loading the module opens no database and reaches no network, and it signs, issues and anchors nothing. It is not an EPCIS repository and has no capture interface ([durable EPCIS import](docs/packages/dpp-credentials.md#durable-epcis-import)).

- `pullEpcisEvents` in `@bsv/dpp-credentials/epcis-exchange` is the query pull of `epcis-json@1`: one bounded run against the standard events query, `GET /events`, of a configured source, over a transport and storage the caller supplies. A source has a `sourceId`, a `baseUrl`, its `permittedOrigins`, a `policy` with the limits in force and `credentials`, a function that obtains the credential. The query selects `matchAnyEPC`, `eventTypes`, `eventTime`, `recordTime.from`, `eventIds` and `perPage`, sent under their standard names with `GS1-EPCIS-Version` and `GS1-CBV-Version`. Every page goes through `importEpcisSource` with `query` provenance, and the checkpoint moves only in the batch that records a page after its import is durable, so a restart or a crash continues without losing, skipping or repeating a page. A cursor that has expired, was rejected or is missing leads to one bounded replay of the same pass, which recognises what is held. A later pass begins at the watermark less `recordTimeOverlapSeconds`, and a query with `eventIds` refreshes known events. New beside it: `getEpcisPull`, `listEpcisPullPages`, `epcisQueryParameters`, `parseNextLink`, `isPrivateHost`, `EPCIS_PULL_LIMIT_CEILINGS`, `EPCIS_PULL_REASON_OUTCOME`, `EPCIS_WIRE_VERSION` and `CBV_WIRE_VERSION`. It is a client of the query interface, not an EPCIS repository: it captures nothing, subscribes to nothing and promises no complete history ([EPCIS query pull](docs/packages/dpp-credentials.md#epcis-query-pull)).
- A pull answers one of the eight source outcomes with a `reason` that names the cause and a `completion` of `exhausted`, `stopped-at-limit` or `failed`, and its `completenessLimits` are never empty. Every address, each redirect and each next page, is checked against the permitted origins before it is requested, redirects are followed by hand, and a credential goes to the base address's origin only and is written to no record, result, log entry or error. A body is counted as it arrives and abandoned at its limit, an HTML page with status 200 is `unexpected-media-type`, and plain `http` and private addresses are refused unless `localTest` is set, which every result records. An operator's limit past its ceiling is refused, and each override is recorded. An unquoted `rel=next`, a numeric page token, a missing `GS1-CBV-Version`, an empty page after a full last page and an unreadable cursor expiry are accepted and recorded ([pull events from a source](docs/interoperability/epcis.md#pull-events-from-a-source)).
- `importEpcisSource` accepts `query` provenance beside `file`: the origin, the standard query parameters that were sent and the page number, and nothing else. `EpcisProvenance` is a union of `EpcisFileProvenance` and `EpcisQueryProvenance`, and the import record of a page reads `complete: false`. `contracts/epcis-exchange-records.schema.json` gains the `epcis-pull` and `epcis-pull-page` records, `EPCIS_RECORD_KINDS` gains `pull` and `pullPage`, and `checkEpcisExchangeInvariants` holds every acknowledged page to its page record and its import.
- `examples/pull-epcis.mjs` pulls the fixture's events from a stand-in source it starts on the local machine, and `scripts/epcis-pull-interop.mjs` runs one pull against an endpoint named in `EPCIS_INTEROP_ENDPOINT` and writes a JSON report. The script is not part of `npm test` and refuses to run without an endpoint.
