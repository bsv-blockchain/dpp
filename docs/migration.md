# Migrating to record version 2 and the managed-custody profile

**Status: informative.** What a deployment does, in what order, to move from the version 1 record and its custody arrangements to the September 2026 release sets, of which [`../release/dpp-release-2026-09-3.json`](https://github.com/bsv-blockchain/dpp/blob/main/release/dpp-release-2026-09-3.json) is the current candidate and the earlier two are its superseded history: record version 2 ([`../spec/record-model-v2.md`](https://github.com/bsv-blockchain/dpp/blob/main/spec/record-model-v2.md)) and the managed-custody profile ([`../spec/managed-custody.md`](https://github.com/bsv-blockchain/dpp/blob/main/spec/managed-custody.md)) on the BSV stack baseline of Node 22, `@bsv/sdk` 2.4.2 and `@bsv/overlay` 2.3.1. Nothing here rewrites a byte already on chain: every version 1 state stays a version 1 state, read under its own rules forever, and a lineage changes version only through the one upgrade transition its holder makes.

## What does not change

Version 1 records, their fixtures, the native claim, the anchor, the verification report's shape and version, the overlay's routes and the publisher policy are as they were. A version 1 reader keeps reading version 1 lineages. A version 1 index keeps admitting version 1 states. The upgrade adds a second layout and a second set of rules beside the first; it deprecates nothing and refuses nothing that version 1 accepted.

## The order of rollout

1. **Readers and verifiers first.** Consume `@bsv/dpp-core` 0.3.0. A reader that does not know version 2 finds no DPP output in a seventeen-field transaction and reports the encoding failed, which is the correct answer for an unsupported version and the reason readers go first: a lineage upgraded before its readers moved would read as broken to them, honestly but unhelpfully. `examples/verify-passport.mjs --fixture --version=2 --report` is the check that a reader is ready.
2. **Indexes next.** Run the image of this repository at the set's version of `@bsv/dpp-overlay-topics` (0.4.0; the first version to admit version 2 was the previous minor), from the preset in [`../deploy/`](https://github.com/bsv-blockchain/dpp/blob/main/deploy/). Unset, `ACCEPTANCE_COMMITMENT` admits version 2 states under the record model's baseline; `required` selects the managed-custody profile, under which a version 2 `TRANSFER` needs its acceptance commitment. Name in `CONTROL_AUTHORITIES` any identity the writer names in its own. An index upgraded before its writers changes nothing for them.
3. **Writers and the service.** Move the application service to the stack baseline and the packages of the set. New passports issue under version 2 only when the deployment selects it (`RECORD_VERSION=2` in the reference application); until then the writer keeps issuing version 1 and reading both. Under version 2 every non-genesis state proves control of the one it spends, so a party that wrote entries to records it did not control (a service member, an automated actor) either becomes a named control authority or moves its assertions to the attestation rail, which needs no spend.
4. **Upgrades, one lineage at a time, by its holder.** The upgrade is a version 2 `UPDATE` that keeps the controller key and the payload and names the version 1 genesis and tip (`spec/record-model-v2.md` §6). The reference application's `upgrade` writes it through the same journal as every other state, so a retry, a crash and a restart resume rather than repeat; the fixtures pin the transition over the version 1 chain and three refusals of it. A lineage need never upgrade: a version 1 record stays readable, transferable under its own profile and closable with its version 1 operations. It upgrades when its holder wants version 2's transfer evidence, or wants to retire it, because `RETIRE` is a version 2 operation.

## Rehearsing on a copy

The reference application's suites run the whole path on the in-memory store with the offline wallet and index (`packages/dpp-service/test/handover-v2.test.ts`: issue, entry, offer, claim, retire, and a version 1 record upgraded then transferred). A deployment rehearses on a copy of its own data the same way: restore the database into a throwaway instance, point a writer at it with `RECORD_VERSION=2` and an index under the profile, upgrade a handful of lineages, transfer one, retire one, and verify each from the index's bytes with the reader of step 1. The original store is never the rehearsal's store, and no product identifier is regenerated: an upgraded lineage keeps its passport identifier and its genesis.

## Rolling back

A reader or index rolled back to a version that does not know version 2 reads every version 2 state as an unsupported encoding and admits none; the states exist on chain regardless, so a rollback of a writer after version 2 records exist is a rollback of new writes and nothing else. Keep readers at 0.3.0 or later and stop the writer from issuing or upgrading (`RECORD_VERSION` unset), and every record stays verifiable. A rollback cannot erase a lineage's version 2 states, and a database reset would not either: it would only make the index forget bytes a stranger can still fetch from the chain.

## What a consumer changes in code

`DppState` is now the union of the version 1 and version 2 states; code that switched on `state.op` alone switches on `state.version` first where the operations differ, and the seven version 1 operation names are joined by `ISSUE`, `UPDATE`, `TRANSFER` and `RETIRE`. `checkTransition` takes a link context (the spent output's index, the lineage genesis and the control authorities), `verifyChain` and `verifyPassportEvidence` take `controlAuthorities` and `managedAcceptance`, and the acceptance record's functions are new. Every version 1 export is unchanged. `Beef.toBinaryAtomic` in `@bsv/sdk` 2.4.2 serialises the subject and its unproven ancestry only, so a writer that announces a proven state beside its proven predecessor frames the atomic prefix over the whole BEEF itself ([`stack.md`](stack.md)).

## The interoperability profiles and the successor industry profiles

The second candidate set, [`../release/dpp-release-2026-09-2.json`](https://github.com/bsv-blockchain/dpp/blob/main/release/dpp-release-2026-09-2.json), adds four optional profiles and two draft industry successors and changes no wire contract: the record, the native claim, the anchor, the acceptance record and the verification report are the first set's. A deployment adopts each profile by configuration, in this order.

1. **Readers and capability declarations first.** Consume `@bsv/dpp-profiles` 0.3.0 and `@bsv/vsc` 0.2.0. A reader that meets the `vc-di-ecdsa-rdfc-2019@1` representation without the new verifier reports it `representation-unsupported`, and a reader that meets a `battery@3` payload without the successor manifest reports the profile unknown; both are the named results the standard requires, never a wrong positive. Capability documents name what is configured: the registry's `GET /capabilities` lists the external representation and, when a resolver origin is configured, the discovery profile, and carries the two readiness statements (EN 18223 serialisation and the Union registry adapter are not implemented) verbatim.
2. **Stores next, additively.** The application adds collections for imports, retained source bytes, checkpoints, publications, source revisions, current pointers and relationships, and the registry adds resolution records; every index is new and no existing collection is reshaped. Rehearse on a copy: restore a database into a throwaway instance, import one document, register one model revision, project one passport and export the source, then compare the exported bytes with the original by digest.
3. **Discovery.** Configure a resolver origin for the registry (`RESOLVER_ORIGIN`) and register resolution records under the namespaces the operator administers; the legacy evidence route on the API origin keeps answering, and the alias `/resolve/gs1/01/{gtin}/21/{serial}` answers identically, so no printed code and no client changes. A deployment that names an external GS1-Conformant Resolver instead runs no resolver of its own and reads it through the same discovery client.
4. **Source exchange.** Configure sources and publication policies for the application (`EPCIS_SOURCES`, `EPCIS_PUBLICATION_POLICIES`). Import and pull default to validation and retention; publication of a mapped event is a separate, authorised action and issues under the existing VSC profile through the registry. No import ever writes a token state.
5. **Successor profiles, opt-in.** `battery@3` and `textile@3` are draft until a reviewed cutover. A writer selects one by explicit version; the application default stays `battery@2` and `textile@2`, whose bytes, generated schemas and consumer documents are unchanged. A record under a successor never reinterprets a record under its predecessor, and the migration block in each successor manifest names the outcome for every predecessor field. The cutover, which marks the predecessor superseded and changes its frozen bytes, is a later reviewed re-freeze and is not part of this set.

**Rolling back** disables new intake, discovery selection or projection routes by unsetting their configuration; retained sources, revisions and credentials stay readable, and readers keep the new packages. Nothing here can unwind an anchor, erase a token transition or reopen a retired lineage, and a source deleted under a retention policy becomes unavailable for later verification while its digest and anchor facts keep their meaning.

## The complete export

The third candidate set, [`../release/dpp-release-2026-09-3.json`](https://github.com/bsv-blockchain/dpp/blob/main/release/dpp-release-2026-09-3.json), changes one package and no wire format: `@bsv/dpp-overlay-topics` 0.4.0 serves the complete export (`GET /evidence-export`, `contracts/overlay.yaml` 0.7.0-draft, `contracts/evidence-export.schema.json`), and the other three packages are the second set's. An operator adopts it by upgrading the index and, if the deployment serves long lineages to strangers, setting `EXPORT_TOKEN` so the complete export sits behind a bearer while the bounded package and the history pages stay open. A reader adopts it by joining parts with `joinEvidenceExport` from the overlay package, or by reproducing the join rules of `spec/portable-evidence.md` §2 in its own code. A package produced by the earlier index is read exactly as it was, bounded and saying so; nothing about it is reinterpreted. The set also names the selection that qualifies it, `conformance/selections/dpp-release-2026-09-3.json`, and a deployment that repeats the qualification sees which claims the set withholds by name.
